Playing a round

It only takes one person to pull the whole group in. Whoever starts the round picks the course and gets a room code and a QR. The rest scan it or type the code and they're in.

From there, one person can put in one bet or a hundred. Everyone who wants a piece of the action needs the app, and everyone sees the same ledger, live, the whole round.

Shot outcomes, hole by hole. Up and down from the bunker. A three-putt blowup. Birdie on the par 5. Fairway off the first tee. "$20 says he doesn't break 90."

The stuff you'd actually say on the tee, written the way you'd say it: "You bet Mike $50 that Mike misses the fairway."

Two ways. If the scorecard can see it, it settles itself. The moment the hole's scores go in, a birdie bet or a double-bogey bet is auto-resolved. No tapping.

If the scorecard can't see it, like an up and down, you tap the bet on the live ledger and mark it won, lost, or void.

Either way, every bet goes on the record, every dollar sits on one shared ledger, and the who-won-the-front argument is dead.

The 19th Hole. The scorecard gets confirmed, the app nets every bet down to one number per pair ("Mike owes you $35"), and you get a one-tap Venmo request for each one.

Money moves on Venmo, between you two, not through us.
